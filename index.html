She is not comparing dresses. She is choosing where to shop.

Questions

The things store owners ask first.

Do you need the physical garment?

No. Nothing ships. We work from the product photography already live on your store. A front and a back image gives the best result, because the back view is the one your supplier photos usually get right and your competitors never show. A single front shot still works.

Will this slow my product page down?

No. Shopify's own limit for a product video is 1 GB and 10 minutes. These clips run a few megabytes and a few seconds, so they are nowhere near it. The widely quoted 20 MB cap is for the Files page, not the product gallery.

Shopify also re-encodes every uploaded video through its own delivery pipeline and streams 480p, 720p or 1080p depending on the shopper's connection. What we hand you is already in line with what Shopify would serve anyway, so compressing it further before upload gains nothing.

The bigger factor for real page speed is how the video is embedded. Native Shopify product media adds no extra script. Some hover-video and shoppable-video apps do, and that is usually where the load time actually goes.

I already get model photos from my wholesaler. Why do I need video?

Those photos do their job, and the video is built from them, so the model and styling stay consistent with what is already on your page. What changes is that the garment moves.

That matters because a page of stills gets scanned and a page of moving garments gets watched. Product pages with rotating imagery hold attention 50 to 80% longer, and studies comparing rotating to static imagery of the same product cluster at 27 to 35% higher conversion. She can see the hem swing, how the fabric falls, what the back looks like in motion. The questions a still leaves open.

There is a second reason worth knowing. Those four images ship with the style, so every boutique carrying that piece is running the same frames. The video is the one asset on your product page that nobody else selling it will have.

Will this actually change my numbers?

The measured effects show up in three places, all upstream of revenue.

Time on page. Moving product imagery roughly doubles engagement time against static galleries, with published figures at 50 to 80% longer. A shopper who lingers on one product page is a shopper who opens another.

Conversion. Rotating versus static imagery on the same product lands at 27 to 35% higher conversion across published studies. Shoppers who watch product video are reported to convert at two to four times the rate of those who do not.

Basket size. The average fashion order is 1.74 items. Anything that keeps a shopper browsing longer has an outsized effect on that number, because the second item is usually found rather than searched for.

Two honest notes. These are published benchmarks from other retailers, not a forecast for your store, and results depend on your traffic, your pricing and where the video sits in your gallery. The link from longer browsing to a bigger basket is well established directionally but harder to attribute cleanly than the conversion figures. What is not in dispute is that motion holds attention longer than stills, and attention is the input everything else on the page depends on.

What would it cost to produce this myself?

Video is priced very differently from stills. A studio product video runs $2,000 to $10,000 per SKU. Batched production brings that to $200 to $800. The cheapest route in the industry, template-based asset swapping at high volume, still lands at $80 to $150 per SKU.

Scale is why most catalogs do not have it. One industry breakdown puts it plainly: shooting dedicated video for 500 SKUs at $3,000 each is a $1.5 million commitment. So brands cover their top sellers and leave everything else static.

Yeltis is $14.97 per garment on a catalog build, and it works from photography you already own. Nothing ships, nothing gets scheduled, and no one has to be available in October.

Extra garments are billed at $35 each, so an unusually big month never blocks a launch or forces an upgrade mid-cycle. If it keeps happening you can move up a tier, and you can change tiers once a quarter in either direction with no penalty.

What format do the files come in?

Whatever your theme uses. Shopify does not enforce a single format across themes, and the shape your product gallery expects depends on which one you run and how it is configured. Themes like Dawn, Refresh and Sense let you choose, while others default to square and crop anything that is not.

So we check your store before we render, and cut every file to match. That way it sits at exactly the same size as the product images already in your gallery, with no cropping, no letterboxing and no theme surgery on your end. Vertical either way, because that is what fills a phone screen, and most of your traffic is on one.
